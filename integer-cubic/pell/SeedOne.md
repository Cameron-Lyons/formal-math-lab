# The cubic candidates through (1,0,1)

Neither of the two mod-7 cubic candidates on the Pell conic $u^2 - 2v^2 = 1$ descends to a rational polynomial substitution. The endpoint argument below rules out both residue classes directly. Independently, an exact elimination rules out every pair of rational cubics $C, J$ and quadratic $E$ solving

$$
2 C^3 + 2 J^2 + (u^2 - 1) E^2 J = 4, \qquad C(1) = J(1) = 1.
$$

Here "cubic" means exact degree 3. The elimination allows arbitrary rational coefficients (including denominators divisible by 7) and works for $u^2 - Dv^2 = 1$ with any nonzero rational $D$, because replacing $1/2$ by $1/D$ in $(u^2 - 1) E(u)^2 / 2$ only rescales the squared quadratic.

## The endpoint at $u = -1$

Set $L = 1 - 2t^2$, $u = (1 + 2t^2)/L$, and $v = 2t/L$, and write the coordinates as $(X, Y, Z)/L^3$. Fixing the seed $(1, 0, 1)$ at $t = 0$, the mod-7 enumeration in [seed_one_endpoint.py](seed_one_endpoint.py) finds only two solutions with $\gcd(\bar X, \bar L) = 1$, differing by the sign of $Y$. Both full Jacobians have determinant 6 modulo 7.

The involution $(X(t), Y(t), Z(t)) \mapsto (X(-t), -Y(-t), Z(-t))$ fixes the equations, the seed, and each residue class. Uniqueness of Hensel lifting forces $X$ and $Z$ to be even and $Y$ to be odd. Because an odd polynomial of degree at most 6 has zero $t^6$ term, $y = 0$ at $t = \infty$. See [CubicEndpoint.lean](CubicEndpoint.lean) for the Lean symmetry and endpoint lemmas.

At $t = \infty$, the conic passes through $(u, v) = (-1, 0)$. In the reduced model, both classes satisfy

$$
C(u) \equiv 4 + 4u^3, \qquad J(u) \equiv 1 + u^2 + 6u^3 \pmod 7.
$$

So the rational point $(-x, z)$ on $Y^2 = X^3 + 2$ at $u = -1$ must reduce modulo 7 to $(0, 3)$. Because $Y^2 = X^3 + 2$ has discriminant $-1728$, reduction modulo 7 is a group homomorphism. Its Mordell-Weil group is generated by $(-1, 1)$ (an external arithmetic fact from [LMFDB curve 1728a1](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/1728a1/)). Modulo 7, $(-1, 1)$ reduces to $(6, 1)$, whose double is $(6, 6) = -(6, 1)$, so its subgroup contains only $\infty$ and $(6, \pm 1)$ and never hits $(0, 3)$. Lean verifies this finite subgroup obstruction in [CubicEndpoint.lean](CubicEndpoint.lean).

## Exact elimination for the reduced identity

Let $\theta$ be a root of $J$ and set $\alpha = C(\theta)$, so $\alpha^3 = 2$. Because $J$ has degree 3 and $T^3 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, $J$ is irreducible and $\mathbb{Q}(\theta) = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Write $\theta = c + i\alpha + j\alpha^2$.

When $ij \ne 0$, put $w = j/i$, change variables to $s = w(u - c)/i$, and write $b$ for the value of $s$ at $u = 1$. Define

$$
P(s) = s^3 - 6w^3 s - 2w^3 - 4w^6, \quad G(s) = s - s^2 + 4w^3, \quad d = w(1 - 2w^3).
$$

At $u = \theta$, $\alpha^3 = 2$ gives $P(s) = 0$ and $G(s) = d\alpha$ (verified in [CubicEliminant.lean](CubicEliminant.lean)). Since $1/2$ is not a rational cube, $d \ne 0$. Putting $A = P(b)$ and $H = G(b) - d$, interpolation at the seed $s = b$ yields

$$
J = \frac{P}{A}, \qquad C = \frac{AG - HP}{dA}.
$$

Both $A$ and $H$ are nonzero ($A \ne 0$ since $J$ is irreducible, and $H \ne 0$ since $\deg C = 3$).

To form $(2 - C^3)/J - J$, set $k = d^3$, $Q = (2k - G^3)/P$, and

$$
F = A^3 Q + 3 A^2 H G^2 - 3 A H^2 G P + H^3 P^2 - AkP.
$$

Multiplying by $P$ checks $PF = 2kA^3 - (AG - HP)^3 - AkP^2$, so after the affine variable change $F = k A^2 (u^2 - 1) E(u)^2 / 2$. Since $s - b$ is proportional to $u - 1$, the quotient $K = F/(s - b)$ is a quintic with leading coefficient $H^3 \ne 0$ that is divisible by the square of a quadratic.

Because $\deg\gcd(K, \partial_s K) \ge 2$, the Sylvester matrix of $K$ and $\partial_s K$ has corank at least 2 at any solution $(w, b)$, so all its cofactors vanish and both the determinant and its $b$-derivative vanish there. Lean proves this Sylvester rank bound and double-root implication in [SeedOneDiscriminant.lean](SeedOneDiscriminant.lean).

Since the leading coefficient of $K$ is nonzero, $\operatorname{disc}_s(K)$ and its $b$-derivative vanish too. Exact integer factorization gives

$$
\operatorname{disc}_s(K) = \text{const} \cdot w^8 (2w^3 - 1)^4 A^4 H^6 \, \Delta(w, b),
$$

where $\Delta$ has bidegree $(68, 34)$ and all factors in front of $\Delta$ are nonzero. Thus $\Delta = \partial_b \Delta = 0$. Because the leading coefficient of $\Delta$ in $b$ is $\pm 4$, $w$ must be a root of $\operatorname{disc}_b \Delta$, a polynomial of degree 2244 factoring (up to a nonzero rational constant) as

$$
w^{330} (2w^3 - 1)^{442} P_{93}(w) P_{90}(w)^2 P_{105}(w)^3.
$$

Homogenized, $P_{93}, P_{90}, P_{105}$ have no projective roots modulo $5, 11, 7$ respectively, and $2w^3 - 1$ has no projective root modulo 7. That leaves only $w = 0$, contradicting $ij \ne 0$. [seed_one_elimination.py](seed_one_elimination.py) recomputes both discriminants, multiplies the factors back together, and matches the full integer coefficients to the Lean rational-root certificate in [SeedOneEliminant.lean](SeedOneEliminant.lean).

### Exceptional generators

If only one of $i, j$ is nonzero, an affine change of variable moves the root to $\alpha^e$ ($e = 1$ or $2$), with seed value $a$. Plugging

$$
P = s^3 - 2^e, \quad A = a^3 - 2^e, \quad G = s^e, \quad d = e, \quad H = a^e - e
$$

into the same formulas gives a quintic $F/(s - a)$ that must contain a squared quadratic. Its discriminant in $s$ factors as below, where each pair lists the degree of a nonlinear factor and a prime at which its homogenization has no projective root:

| $e$ | Only factor with a rational root | Remaining factors (degree, prime) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $(a - 1)^6$ | $(3, 7)$, $(18, 3)$ |
| 2 | $a - 2$ | $(2, 3)$, $(3, 7)$, $(6, 5)$, $(27, 3)$ |

When $e = 1$ and $a = 1$, $H = 0$ gives $C = s$, violating $\deg C = 3$. When $e = 2$ and $a = 2$, the quintic is a scalar multiple of $s^2 (s^3 - 4s^2 + 4s - 12)$, whose gcd with its derivative is just $s$, ruling out a squared quadratic (proved via an explicit Bézout identity in [SeedOneEliminant.lean](SeedOneEliminant.lean), matching the factorizations in [seed_one_elimination.py](seed_one_elimination.py)). This finishes every generator of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.

## Denominator cases at the two earlier seeds

For $D = 3$, let $h = \max_{i > 0}(-v_7(a_i)/i) > 0$ over the nonconstant numerator coefficients of $X, Y, Z$. Writing $h = m/n$ in lowest terms and scaling $t$ by $7^{m/n}$ in a totally ramified extension makes all coefficients 7-integral with at least one surviving unit coefficient at a positive index divisible by $n$. Every nonconstant residue triple has surviving indices of gcd 1, forcing $n = 1$. The rescaled denominator is $Q = 1 - 3 \cdot 7^{2h} t^2$, which leaves only two cases at precisions $7^3$ and $7^4$: $h = 1$ and $h \ge 2$.

The table counts how many initial mod-7 residue triples lift to each precision (the same counts hold for both $h = 1$ and $h \ge 2$), checked in [seeded_denominator_lifts.py](seeded_denominator_lifts.py):

| Seed | Modulo 7 | Modulo 49 | Modulo 343 | Modulo 2401 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $(0, 1, 1)$ | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| $(-2, 3, 1)$ | 42 | 42 | 36 | 36 |

Every initial residue has Jacobian rank 15 and 343 lifts modulo 49. For the 6 excluded residues through $(-2, 3, 1)$, all 343 first lifts fail modulo 343. For 24 of the remaining residues through $(-2, 3, 1)$, only 16,807 of the 117,649 mod-343 lifts continue to 2401. In total, 42 initial classes survive these finite tests.

The 6 excluded residues form a single orbit under $t \mapsto ut$ ($u \in \mathbb{F}_7^\times$). On a representative, the first-lift equations pin the digits of $X_6, Y_5, Y_6$ to $6, 1, 0$, after which the $t^{16}$ coefficient of the residual divided by 49 is $3 \bmod 7$. This blocks lifting modulo 343 for every denominator $Q = 1 + 49ct^2$, where arbitrary $c$ covers the rescaling across the orbit; see [SeedDenominatorObstruction.lean](SeedDenominatorObstruction.lean).

## Reproduction

```sh
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seed_one_endpoint.py
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seed_one_elimination.py
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seeded_denominator_lifts.py
lake -d .. build
```

The reduction to the quintic, discriminant factorizations, and denominator counts rely on the written proof above and exact Python/FLINT computations. Separately, [PellAmplification.lean](PellAmplification.lean) proves in Lean that any rational substitution of this form with nonconstant $C$ would generate infinitely many integer solutions via a subsequence of powers of $3 + 2\sqrt{2}$.
