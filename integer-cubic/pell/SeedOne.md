# The cubic candidates through $(1, 0, 1)$

In the search for **cubic** Pell substitutions for $z^2 + y^2 z + x^3 = 2$ (where $x$ has pole order $3$ at infinity), an exhaustive mod-$7$ census on the Pell conic $u^2 - 2v^2 = 1$ through the integer seed $(1, 0, 1)$ leaves exactly two candidate residue classes. This note proves that **neither candidate descends to a rational polynomial substitution**, and gives two independent obstructions:

1. **Elliptic-curve endpoint obstruction at $u = -1$:** Symmetry and Hensel uniqueness force any rational lift of the two mod-$7$ candidates to produce a rational point on $Y^2 = X^3 + 2$ whose mod-$7$ reduction lies outside the image of the curve's rational Mordell–Weil group.
2. **Exact height-free elimination over $\mathbb{Q}$:** Independently, exact discriminant elimination rules out **every** pair of exact-degree-$3$ rational cubics $C, J$ and quadratic $E$ solving the reduced identity
   $$
   2 C^3 + 2 J^2 + (u^2 - 1) E^2 J = 4, \qquad C(1) = J(1) = 1.
   $$
   Here "cubic" means exact degree $3$. This elimination allows arbitrary rational coefficients (including denominators divisible by $7$) and holds on **every** Pell conic $u^2 - Dv^2 = 1$ with nonzero rational $D$, because replacing $1/2$ by $1/D$ in $(u^2 - 1)E(u)^2/2$ merely rescales the squared quadratic.

We also record finite $7$-adic lifting counts for denominator cases at the two earlier seeds $(0, 1, 1)$ and $(-2, 3, 1)$ on $u^2 - 3v^2 = 1$, together with a Lean-certified mod-$343$ obstruction for one $6$-element orbit.

## Proof 1: The endpoint obstruction at $u = -1$

Parametrize $u^2 - 2v^2 = 1$ by $L = 1 - 2t^2$, $u = (1 + 2t^2)/L$, and $v = 2t/L$, and write the coordinates as $(X, Y, Z)/L^3$. Fixing the seed $(1, 0, 1)$ at $t = 0$, the mod-$7$ enumeration in [seed_one_endpoint.py](seed_one_endpoint.py) finds only **two** solutions with $\gcd(\bar X, \bar L) = 1$, differing by the sign of $Y$. Both full Jacobians have determinant $6 \pmod 7$ (invertible).

1. **Symmetry forces $y = 0$ at $u = -1$.** The involution $(X(t), Y(t), Z(t)) \mapsto (X(-t), -Y(-t), Z(-t))$ fixes the polynomial equations, the seed, and each of the two residue classes. Uniqueness of Hensel lifting therefore forces $X$ and $Z$ to be even polynomials and $Y$ to be odd. Because an odd polynomial of degree at most $6$ has zero $t^6$ coefficient, we get $y = 0$ at $t = \infty$, where the conic passes through $(u, v) = (-1, 0)$. See [CubicEndpoint.lean](CubicEndpoint.lean) for the Lean symmetry and endpoint lemmas.
2. **Mod-7 coordinates at the endpoint.** In the reduced model, both classes satisfy
   $$
   C(u) \equiv 4 + 4u^3, \qquad J(u) \equiv 1 + u^2 + 6u^3 \pmod 7.
   $$
   Evaluating at $u = -1$ gives $x = C(-1) \equiv 0$ and $z = J(-1) \equiv 3 \pmod 7$.
3. **Elliptic-curve subgroup obstruction.** Since $y = 0$ at $u = -1$, the pair $(-x, z)$ is a rational point on the elliptic curve $Y^2 = X^3 + 2$ that must reduce modulo $7$ to $(0, 3)$. Because $Y^2 = X^3 + 2$ has discriminant $-1728$ (good reduction at $7$), reduction modulo $7$ is a group homomorphism. Its Mordell–Weil group over $\mathbb{Q}$ is generated by $(-1, 1)$ (an external arithmetic fact from [LMFDB curve 1728a1](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/1728a1/)). Modulo $7$, $(-1, 1)$ reduces to $(6, 1)$, whose double is $(6, 6) = -(6, 1)$. Thus its subgroup contains only $\infty$ and $(6, \pm 1)$ and never hits $(0, 3)$.

Lean verifies this finite subgroup obstruction in [CubicEndpoint.lean](CubicEndpoint.lean).

## Proof 2: Exact elimination for the reduced identity

To rule out $2C^3 + 2J^2 + (u^2 - 1)E^2 J = 4$ for arbitrary rational coefficients (even with denominators divisible by $7$), we use the arithmetic of the cubic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ to parameterize all valid pairs $(C, J)$ by two rational parameters $(w, b)$, turn the quadratic factor $E^2$ into a repeated-root condition on a quintic polynomial $K$, and apply two successive discriminants to eliminate $s$ and $b$.

### Step 1: Parameterizing $(C, J)$ via $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$

Let $\theta$ be a root of $J$ and set $\alpha = C(\theta)$. Since $J(\theta) = 0$, the surface identity forces $\alpha^3 = 2$. Because $\deg J = 3$ and $T^3 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, $J$ is irreducible and $\mathbb{Q}(\theta) = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Write
$$
\theta = c + i\alpha + j\alpha^2 \qquad (c, i, j \in \mathbb{Q}).
$$

### Step 2: Generic generators ($ij \ne 0$)

Put $w = j/i$, change variables to $s = w(u - c)/i$, and write $b$ for the value of $s$ at the seed $u = 1$. Define
$$
P(s) = s^3 - 6w^3 s - 2w^3 - 4w^6, \qquad G(s) = s - s^2 + 4w^3, \qquad d = w(1 - 2w^3).
$$
At $u = \theta$, the relation $\alpha^3 = 2$ gives $P(s) = 0$ and $G(s) = d\alpha$ (verified in [CubicEliminant.lean](CubicEliminant.lean)). Since $1/2$ is not a rational cube, $d \ne 0$.

Setting $A = P(b)$ and $H = G(b) - d$, interpolation at the seed $s = b$ (where $J = C = 1$) gives
$$
J = \frac{P}{A}, \qquad C = \frac{AG - HP}{dA}.
$$
Both $A$ and $H$ are nonzero ($A \ne 0$ because $J$ is irreducible, and $H \ne 0$ because $\deg C = 3$).

### Step 3: Reduction to a quintic with a squared quadratic factor

To form $(2 - C^3)/J - J$, set $k = d^3$, $Q = (2k - G^3)/P$, and
$$
F = A^3 Q + 3 A^2 H G^2 - 3 A H^2 G P + H^3 P^2 - AkP.
$$
Multiplying by $P$ confirms $PF = 2kA^3 - (AG - HP)^3 - AkP^2$, so after the affine variable change $F = k A^2 (u^2 - 1) E(u)^2 / 2$. Since $s - b$ is proportional to $u - 1$, the quotient
$$
K = \frac{F}{s - b}
$$
is a quintic polynomial in $s$ with leading coefficient $H^3 \ne 0$ that must be divisible by the square of a quadratic.

### Step 4: Two successive discriminants force $w = 0$

Because $\deg\gcd(K, \partial_s K) \ge 2$, the Sylvester matrix of $K$ and $\partial_s K$ has corank at least $2$ at any solution $(w, b)$. Consequently, all its cofactors vanish, so both the determinant and its $b$-derivative vanish there. Lean proves this Sylvester rank bound and double-root implication in [SeedOneDiscriminant.lean](SeedOneDiscriminant.lean).

Since the leading coefficient $H^3$ of $K$ is nonzero, $\operatorname{disc}_s(K)$ and its $b$-derivative vanish as well. Exact integer factorization yields
$$
\operatorname{disc}_s(K) = \text{const} \cdot w^8 (2w^3 - 1)^4 A^4 H^6 \, \Delta(w, b),
$$
where $\Delta$ has bidegree $(68, 34)$ and every factor in front of $\Delta$ is nonzero. Thus $\Delta = \partial_b \Delta = 0$.

Because the leading coefficient of $\Delta$ in $b$ is $\pm 4$, $w$ must be a root of $\operatorname{disc}_b \Delta$—a degree-$2244$ polynomial factoring (up to a nonzero rational constant) as
$$
w^{330} (2w^3 - 1)^{442} P_{93}(w) P_{90}(w)^2 P_{105}(w)^3.
$$
Homogenized:
* $P_{93}$, $P_{90}$, and $P_{105}$ have no projective roots modulo $5$, $11$, and $7$, respectively; and
* $2w^3 - 1$ has no projective root modulo $7$.

Only $w = 0$ survives, contradicting $ij \ne 0$. [seed_one_elimination.py](seed_one_elimination.py) recomputes both discriminants, multiplies the factors back together, and matches the full integer coefficients to the Lean rational-root certificate in [SeedOneEliminant.lean](SeedOneEliminant.lean).

### Step 5: Exceptional generators (one of $i, j$ zero)

If only one of $i, j$ is nonzero, an affine change of variable moves the root to $\alpha^e$ ($e = 1$ or $2$), with seed value $a$. Substituting
$$
P = s^3 - 2^e, \qquad A = a^3 - 2^e, \qquad G = s^e, \qquad d = e, \qquad H = a^e - e
$$
into the same formulas gives a quintic $F/(s - a)$ that must contain a squared quadratic. Its discriminant in $s$ factors as follows (each pair lists the degree of a nonlinear factor and a prime modulo which its homogenization has no projective root):

| $e$ | Only factor with a rational root | Remaining factors $(\text{degree}, \text{prime})$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $(a - 1)^6$ | $(3, 7),\; (18, 3)$ |
| 2 | $a - 2$ | $(2, 3),\; (3, 7),\; (6, 5),\; (27, 3)$ |

Checking the two remaining rational roots closes every generator of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$:
* When $e = 1$ and $a = 1$, $H = 0$ gives $C = s$, violating $\deg C = 3$.
* When $e = 2$ and $a = 2$, the quintic is a scalar multiple of $s^2 (s^3 - 4s^2 + 4s - 12)$, whose gcd with its derivative is just $s$—ruling out a squared quadratic (proved via an explicit Bézout identity in [SeedOneEliminant.lean](SeedOneEliminant.lean), matching the factorizations in [seed_one_elimination.py](seed_one_elimination.py)).

## Denominator cases at the two earlier seeds ($D = 3$)

For $D = 3$, let $h = \max_{i > 0}(-v_7(a_i)/i) > 0$ over the nonconstant numerator coefficients of $X, Y, Z$. Writing $h = m/n$ in lowest terms and scaling $t$ by $7^{m/n}$ in a totally ramified extension makes all coefficients $7$-integral with at least one surviving unit coefficient at a positive index divisible by $n$. Every nonconstant residue triple has surviving indices of gcd $1$, forcing $n = 1$. The rescaled denominator is $Q = 1 - 3 \cdot 7^{2h} t^2$, leaving only two cases at precisions $7^3$ and $7^4$: $h = 1$ and $h \ge 2$.

The table counts how many initial mod-$7$ residue triples lift to each precision (identical counts hold for $h = 1$ and $h \ge 2$), checked in [seeded_denominator_lifts.py](seeded_denominator_lifts.py):

| Seed | Modulo $7$ | Modulo $49$ | Modulo $343$ | Modulo $2401$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $(0, 1, 1)$ | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| $(-2, 3, 1)$ | 42 | 42 | 36 | 36 |

* Every initial residue has Jacobian rank $15$ and $343$ lifts modulo $49$.
* For the $6$ excluded residues through $(-2, 3, 1)$, all $343$ first lifts fail modulo $343$.
* For $24$ of the remaining residues through $(-2, 3, 1)$, only $16{,}807$ of the $117{,}649$ mod-$343$ lifts continue to $2401$.
* In total, $42$ initial classes survive these finite tests.

**Lean obstruction for the 6 excluded residues:** These $6$ residues form a single orbit under $t \mapsto ut$ ($u \in \mathbb{F}_7^\times$). On a representative, the first-lift equations pin the digits of $X_6, Y_5, Y_6$ to $6, 1, 0$, after which the $t^{16}$ coefficient of the residual divided by $49$ is $3 \bmod 7$. This blocks lifting modulo $343$ for every denominator $Q = 1 + 49ct^2$, where arbitrary $c$ covers rescaling across the orbit; see [SeedDenominatorObstruction.lean](SeedDenominatorObstruction.lean).

```sh
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seed_one_endpoint.py
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seed_one_elimination.py
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seeded_denominator_lifts.py
lake -d .. build
```

The reduction to the quintic, the discriminant factorizations, and the denominator counts rely on the written proof above and exact Python/FLINT computations. Separately, [PellAmplification.lean](PellAmplification.lean) proves in Lean that any rational substitution of this form with nonconstant $C$ would generate infinitely many integer solutions via a subsequence of powers of $3 + 2\sqrt{2}$.
