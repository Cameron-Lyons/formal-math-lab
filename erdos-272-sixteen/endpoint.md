# Singleton intersections at both endpoints

**Proposition.** Let $t(15)$ denote the unrestricted maximum on fifteen points. In any admissible family of $f\ge125$ subsets of $[16]$, at least $\lceil(f-11-t(15))/2\rceil$ disjoint pairs of members intersect in exactly $\{1\}$. The same holds for $\{16\}$. Since $t(15)=109$, at least three disjoint pairs witness each endpoint when $f=125$. [Fifteen-point bound](../erdos-272-fifteen/)

Consider deletion of the endpoint 1. A *duplicate pair* consists of $P$ and $P\cup\{1\}$ both belonging to the family, with $1\notin P$. Their common trace $P$ is a nonempty progression. We first establish that a family of 125 can have at most 11 such pairs.

There are 284 nonempty progressions $P\subseteq\{2,\ldots,16\}$. For distinct duplicate bases $P,Q$, both $P\cap Q$ and $(P\cap Q)\cup\{1\}$ must be progressions. Use these two conditions as edges of a graph on the 284 bases. Any choice of 12 duplicate bases must be contained in a maximal clique of size at least 12. There are 5,627 such maximal cliques. The verifier enumerates them using the Bron–Kerbosch algorithm with a pivot, checking each as follows. [Python verifier](endpoint.py)

Fix a maximal clique $C$. At most $|C|-12$ bases can be left out when choosing the 12. For a chosen base $P$, each family member must be compatible with both $P$ and $P\cup\{1\}$. A set is allowed to equal either member, since the intersection condition only concerns distinct sets. The verifier first keeps all masks failing this test for at most $|C|-12$ bases. It then tries to exclude the resulting candidate list by counting, by partitioning the candidates into classes of mutually incompatible masks, or by doing the same for the masks omitting a given point. The full list must admit 125 mutually compatible members and each omission list must admit at least 14. If needed, branch on requiring or skipping a base and repeat, decreasing the number of permitted skips in the latter branch. After all required bases have been fixed, both members of each corresponding duplicate pair are already selected. For the remaining candidates the verifier applies the coloring tests with these members subtracted from the targets. It can also branch on a point's omission list. After removing one fewer independent class than the number of omissions still required, any completion must use a remaining mask. The search tries these masks in turn, retaining their neighbors and removing only alternatives already tried at that node. At most three such additional choices suffice to exclude every case. [Proof of the omission bound](general-bound.md)

Now form a graph on the 125 family members whose edges are pairs with intersection exactly $\{1\}$. Let $U$ be any vertex cover. Delete $U$, then remove 1 from every remaining set. All pairwise intersections remain nonempty progressions: removing the endpoint of a progression preserves its common difference, and every intersection that would have become empty was covered. There are at most 11 coincidences in the resulting list, by the duplicate-pair bound. After merging them we obtain at least $125-|U|-11$ distinct sets on fifteen points. Hence $125-|U|-11\le t(15)$. The endpoints of a maximal matching form a vertex cover. Reflection gives the same inequality at the other endpoint.

Run `python3 endpoint.py` in this directory to reproduce the finite part. It checks 5,627 maximal collections in 10,517 branches and rules out twelve duplicate pairs.

For $f\ge125$, twelve duplicate pairs would fit inside a 125-member subfamily, so there are still at most eleven. Thus $f-|U|-11\le t(15)$ for every vertex cover $U$. A maximal matching of size $m$ supplies a cover of size $2m$, giving the claimed bound.
