# The cubic candidates through (1,0,1)

The two candidates on the Pell conic $u^2-2v^2=1$ do not descend to rational polynomial substitutions. The endpoint argument below rules out the two residue classes. An independent elimination excludes every pair of rational cubics $C,J$ and quadratic $E$ satisfying
$$
2C^3+2J^2+(u^2-1)E^2J=4,\qquad C(1)=J(1)=1.
$$
Here “cubic” means degree exactly three. The elimination allows arbitrary rational coefficients, including denominators divisible by 7. It also works for $u^2-Dv^2=1$ with any nonzero rational $D$, since replacing $1/2$ by $1/D$ in the factor $(u^2-1)E(u)^2/2$ only changes the nonzero scalar multiplying the squared quadratic.

## The endpoint at $u=-1$

Set $L=1-2t^2$, $u=(1+2t^2)/L$, $v=2t/L$, and write the polynomial coordinates as $(X,Y,Z)/L^3$. With the seed fixed at $t=0$, an enumeration modulo 7 gives two solutions for which $X$ remains coprime to $L$. They differ by the sign of $Y$. Both Jacobian determinants of the full coefficient system are 6 modulo 7. The operation $(X(t),Y(t),Z(t))\mapsto(X(-t),-Y(-t),Z(-t))$ preserves that system, the seed and each residue class. Uniqueness of a lift in a nonsingular residue class forces the two outside numerators to be even and the middle one to be odd. The odd numerator has zero coefficient in degree six, hence $y=0$ at $t=\infty$. [Enumeration](seed_one_endpoint.py) · [Lean symmetry and endpoint lemmas](CubicEndpoint.lean)

At $t=\infty$ the conic meets $(u,v)=(-1,0)$. Equivalently, in the reduced model both classes give
$$
C(u)\equiv4+4u^3,\qquad J(u)\equiv1+u^2+6u^3\pmod7.
$$
Consequently the rational point $(-x,z)$ on $Y^2=X^3+2$ at this endpoint must reduce to $(0,3)$. The elliptic curve has discriminant $-1728$, so reduction at 7 is a group homomorphism. Its rational points are generated by $(-1,1)$. Modulo 7 this generator is $(6,1)$, whose double is $(6,6)=-(6,1)$. Its multiples are the point at infinity and $(6,\pm1)$. This excludes the required endpoint. The Mordell–Weil generator is an external arithmetic result. Lean verifies the finite subgroup obstruction. [Cremona curve 1728a1](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/1728a1/) · [Lean proof](CubicEndpoint.lean)

## Exact elimination for the reduced identity

Let $\theta$ be a root of $J$ and put $\alpha=C(\theta)$. Then $\alpha^3=2$. Since $J$ is cubic and $T^3-2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, $J$ is irreducible and $\mathbb Q(\theta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Write $\theta=c+i\alpha+j\alpha^2$ in that basis.

When $ij\ne0$, put $w=j/i$, change variable to $s=w(u-c)/i$, and let $b$ be the value of $s$ at $u=1$. Define
$$
P(s)=s^3-6w^3s-2w^3-4w^6,\quad G(s)=s-s^2+4w^3,\quad d=w(1-2w^3).
$$
At $u=\theta$, $P(s)=0$ and $G(s)=d\alpha$, by $\alpha^3=2$. Here $d\ne0$, since $1/2$ is not a rational cube. Write $A=P(b)$ and $H=G(b)-d$. Interpolation at the seed gives
$$
J=P/A,\qquad C=(A G-H P)/(d A).
$$
In particular $A\ne0$ and $H\ne0$, the latter because $C$ has degree three. [Lean cubic-root identity](CubicEliminant.lean)

To compute $(2-C^3)/J-J$, put $k=d^3$, $Q=(2k-G^3)/P$, and form
$$
F=A^3Q+3A^2H G^2-3A H^2 G P+H^3 P^2-AkP.
$$
The exact multiplication check is $PF=2kA^3-(AG-HP)^3-AkP^2$. Thus $F=kA^2(u^2-1)E(u)^2/2$ after the affine change of variable. Since $s-b$ is proportional to $u-1$, the quotient $K=F/(s-b)$ is a quintic divisible by a squared quadratic. Its leading coefficient is $H^3$.

The Sylvester matrix of $K,\partial_s K$ has corank at least two since their gcd has degree at least two. All its cofactors vanish at the putative $(w,b)$, so its determinant and its derivative with respect to $b$ vanish there. Lean proves that a squared quadratic in a specialized quintic gives a double root of this resultant in the parameter. [Sylvester rank bound and quintic resultant](SeedOneDiscriminant.lean)

As the leading coefficient of $K$ is nonzero, the same vanishing holds for $\operatorname{disc}_s(K)$. Exact integer division gives
$$
\operatorname{disc}_s(K)=\text{const}\cdot w^8(2w^3-1)^4 A^4 H^6\,\Delta(w,b),
$$
where $\Delta$ has bidegree $(68,34)$. The removed factors are nonzero. Consequently $\Delta=\partial_b\Delta=0$. Its leading coefficient in $b$ is $\pm4$, so $w$ must be a root of $\operatorname{disc}_b\Delta$.

This last polynomial has degree 2244. Up to a nonzero rational constant it is
$$
w^{330}(2w^3-1)^{442}P_{93}(w)P_{90}(w)^2P_{105}(w)^3.
$$
The homogeneous forms of $P_{93},P_{90},P_{105}$ have no projective root modulo 5, 11, 7 respectively. The factor $2w^3-1$ has no projective root modulo 7. The sole rational possibility is $w=0$, incompatible with $ij\ne0$. The script recomputes both discriminants, verifies the factor product, and compares the full integer coefficients to the input of the Lean rational-root proof. [Exact computation](seed_one_elimination.py) · [Lean root certificate](SeedOneEliminant.lean)

### Exceptional generators

If just one of $i,j$ is nonzero, an affine change of variable takes the root to $\alpha^e$ with $e=1$ or 2. Denote the new value at the seed by $a$. In the same formulas use
$$
P=s^3-2^e,\quad A=a^3-2^e,\quad G=s^e,\quad d=e,\quad H=a^e-e.
$$
The quintic $F/(s-a)$ must again contain a squared quadratic. Its discriminant in $s$ has the following factors. Each pair below gives a degree and a prime for which the homogeneous factor has no projective zero.

| $e$ | Only factor with a rational root | Remaining factors (degree, prime) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $(a-1)^6$ | $(3,7)$, $(18,3)$ |
| 2 | $a-2$ | $(2,3)$, $(3,7)$, $(6,5)$, $(27,3)$ |

For $e=1,a=1$, $H=0$ gives $C=s$, contradicting its degree. For $e=2,a=2$, the quintic is proportional to $s^2(s^3-4s^2+4s-12)$. Its gcd with its derivative is $s$, ruling out a squared quadratic. In Lean an explicit polynomial Bézout identity proves the last exclusion. This exhausts the generators of the cubic field. [Exact factorizations](seed_one_elimination.py) · [Lean exceptional cases](SeedOneEliminant.lean)

## Denominator cases at the two earlier seeds

For $D=3$, let $h=\max_{i>0}(-v_7(a_i)/i)>0$ over the nonconstant numerator coefficients of $X,Y,Z$. Scaling $t$ by a parameter of valuation $h$ makes the coefficients integral and leaves at least one positive-index coefficient a unit. If $h=m/n$ in lowest terms, take $7^{m/n}$ in a totally ramified extension. Only indices divisible by $n$ can survive reduction. The enumeration finds gcd 1 among the surviving indices in every nonconstant residue triple, so $n=1$. The denominator after scaling is $Q=1-3\cdot7^{2h}t^2$. This gives just two possibilities at precision $7^3$ and $7^4$, according as $h=1$ or $h\ge2$.

The columns below count distinct initial residue triples with at least one lift to each precision. Each count holds in both slope cases.

| Seed | Modulo 7 | Modulo 49 | Modulo 343 | Modulo 2401 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $(0,1,1)$ | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| $(-2,3,1)$ | 42 | 42 | 36 | 36 |

Every initial residue has Jacobian rank 15 and 343 lifts modulo 49. For each of the six excluded residues, all 343 corrections fail at the next step. For 24 of the surviving residues through $(-2,3,1)$, only 16,807 of the 117,649 lifts modulo 343 extend to 2401. These finite tests leave 42 initial classes for consideration. [Exhaustive lift checker](seeded_denominator_lifts.py)

The six excluded residues form one orbit under $t\mapsto ut$ for nonzero $u\bmod7$. For a representative, the first correction equations force the digits at $X_6,Y_5,Y_6$ to be $6,1,0$. The coefficient at $t^{16}$ in the surface residual, divided by 49, is then $3\bmod7$. This contradicts a lift modulo 343 for every denominator $Q=1+49c t^2$. Allowing arbitrary $c$ covers the rescaling of the denominator when passing from the representative to another member of the orbit. [Lean degree-16 obstruction](SeedDenominatorObstruction.lean)

```sh
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seed_one_endpoint.py
uv run --with python-flint==0.9.0 seed_one_elimination.py

```

The reduction to the quintic, the discriminant factorization and the denominator counts use the written argument and exact Python/FLINT computations. Lean also proves the conditional infinitude theorem for any rational polynomial substitution of the indicated form with nonconstant $C$, by passing to an integral subsequence of powers of $3+2\sqrt2$.
