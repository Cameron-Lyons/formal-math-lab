# Further exponent compression and quotient-aware F4: static assessment

This note is source analysis only; no new solver build or full solve was run. Source paths are relative to the seventh checkout in `exact_msolve_symbolic_reserve_tools/msolve-cc044551f8e202d00691990fd3fd0b1226a88ea2/src/`. See `exact_msolve_byte_exponent_tools/README.md` for validation and provenance of the checked byte build.

## What the measured matrices show

The last completed matrix in the 3-GiB minimum-fill trial (`exact_msolve_full_native3_minfill_symbolic_3g.log`) had 17,439 rows and 3,200,323 columns. Of those columns, 3,182,900 were nonpivots, leaving only 17,423 known-pivot columns (about 0.5444%). Returned rows used 2,962,178 columns. Because symbolic preprocessing tests every introduced monomial against the active leading ideal, and that ideal contains a divisor of every field cube, every field-reducible monomial lands in the known-pivot block. Over 99.45% of the columns in this matrix are already field-normal, with every coordinate exponent at most 2.

That caps the gain from **only dropping field-reducible columns of that matrix** at 0.55%. A different algorithm that cancels S-polynomial terms before inserting columns could produce a smaller matrix, so this statistic does not rule out algorithmic gains. It does show that field normalization alone will not remove most observed columns.

The run later failed cleanly during a symbolic reservation: load 3,624,513 plus a 1,875,945-term source row plus one spare required 5,500,459 entries, rounded to 5,505,024. Previous confirmed symbolic capacity was 5,177,344. With 97 lanes per vector, the old `uint16_t` principal symbolic arrays would take 1,208.5 MiB at that target, compared with 1,808 MiB for the smallest sufficient power-of-two table. That target allocation failed; this comparison covers one logged request, not process RSS or an equivalent step under a different variable order.

## Storage options beyond one-byte exponents

`neogb/data.h:85` defines `exp_t`, and the vector layout includes a total-degree lane. Switching to one byte is unusually clean because existing allocations and copies already scale by `sizeof(exp_t)`. Sub-byte or sparse formats break direct C indexing: `hash.c`, `order.c`, `symbol.c`, `io.c`, and final reduction in `f4.c` all read `ht->ev[id][coordinate]` directly and cache `exp_t **` pointers.

- **Sparse coordinate lists**: At total degree 5, a sparse list needs at most five `(variable, exponent)` pairs plus degree and count fields (roughly 12 bytes instead of 97). That estimate only holds at low degree. Degree can reach 193 in the 96-variable F3 model, where dense monomials would take more space than a byte vector. A hybrid format requires custom ownership, lookup, comparison, and multiplication routines rather than a typedef swap.
- **Dense 2-bit coordinates with escapes**: For field-normal monomials, 96 coordinates fit in 24 bytes plus a 1-byte degree. All initial non-domain terms and promoted right-block terms have coordinates at most 2, and pure domains and pending pair LCMs have coordinates at most 3. Larger symbolic coordinates still occur, so a checked escape path is mandatory. The claim "symbolic coordinate at most 5" fails under recursive reductions; only the total-degree bound holds generally.
- **Final interreduction caveat**: Final interreduction (`f4.c:572ff`) resets all columns to the right block and calls the legacy all-column importer, which can copy unused intermediate symbolic monomials with larger coordinates into the basis hash. Either an escape format or a separately audited support-only final importer is needed before assuming 2-bit storage covers all basis entries.
- **Dense 4-bit coordinates (nibbles)**: Four-bit coordinates shrink the escape set, but still need runtime checks because no proof here bounds all symbolic coordinates by 15. A checked 4-bit build must fail nonzero or use an exact escape format and never truncate.

Any packed format needs explicit accessors or decode buffers at every exponent read, an audit of stable IDs and pointer lifetimes, exact hashing and comparison agreement, and tests covering staging, rehashing, reset, and copy.

## A sound quotient-aware F4 design

Let `J` be the field ideal generated by all `x_i^3 - x_i`, and let `N` be its canonical monomial normal-form map. Normalizing products is exact modulo `J`, but dropping normalized products into the current F4 loop breaks several assumptions.

The pair selector (`symbol.c:200ff`) groups parents sharing an ordinary LCM, emits one upper row and the rest as lower rows, and marks that LCM as a known pivot. Polynomial multiplication (`hash.c:1548ff`) reuses borrowed source coefficients. Once you normalize modulo `J`, monomials can merge or cancel, leading terms can shift, and normalized parents may no longer supply the recorded LCM pivot. Fresh merged coefficient arrays would also break ownership rules unless integrated into linear algebra (`la_ff_8.c:97ff` distinguishes borrowed reducer coefficients from newly computed ones).

A sound quotient-aware replacement would need to:

1. Keep ordinary critical pairs, including pairs involving every field-domain generator.
2. Build explicit S-polynomials, normalize them modulo `J`, combine colliding monomials, and drop zero terms and zero rows before assembling the matrix.
3. For any field-normal monomial `m` divisible by a non-domain leading monomial `LM(g)`, insert `N((m / LM(g)) * g)`. The leading term `m` survives because normalization only lowers DRL terms.
4. Audit pair criteria, leading-ideal coverage, sugar/degree metadata, coefficient ownership, and zero-row accounting, while keeping `J` available for basis export.
5. Prove that termination establishes the ordinary Buchberger criterion for `J + G` without dropping implicit field critical pairs.

Keeping field pairs is mandatory. Over F3, `{x^3 - x, x^2 + 1}` generates the unit ideal (0 solutions). Dropping the normalized field equation leaves only `x^2 + 1`, where an ordinary pair loop can stop with quotient dimension 2 instead of finding the linear remainder and unit.

Closing the span of normalized generators under multiplication by every variable avoids pair selection, but lives in a `3^96`-dimensional ambient space. Without a sparse on-demand reduction proof, that approach does not scale.

## Recommendation

Evaluate the independently reviewed checked-byte build first. If more memory reduction is still needed, normal-monomial packing with exact escapes has a clear space payoff, though it requires a larger source refactor. Quotient-aware F4 is mathematically workable under the conditions above, but the measured column counts give no evidence that it will save comparable memory. This analysis alone does not justify a ninth build.
